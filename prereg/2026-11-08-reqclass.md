# Requirement classification: under-specified issue or arbitrary tests? (2026-11-08, before any rating)

Answers the main objection of `paper/review-2026-11-08.md`: the paper's "missing contract" may be (a) behaviour the issue leaves out that a developer would need, or (b) details the tests check that nobody could guess. CPU and LLM raters only; research data (`research/reqclass/`), never trained on or shipped.

## Material
62 blind packets (`scripts/build_req_packets.py`): N32 ∪ H35, each with the issue text and the test patch only. No outcomes, arms or gold fix.

## Rating
Each rater lists the behavioural requirements the new or changed tests impose (one per distinct checked behaviour; at most 8 per task) and classifies each:
- **I** stated or directly implied by the issue;
- **R** not in the issue, but a competent developer would infer it from the issue plus the repository's existing code, docs or conventions;
- **C** a defensible choice among reasonable alternatives that the issue leaves open (the PR author decided);
- **A** arbitrary or incidental: exact wording of a message, a name or signature not in the issue and not dictated by the code, a test-only artefact.
Raters: (1) a Claude subagent, (2) Gemma 4 31B on the local server (same rubric, temperature 0.2). Each rates blind to the other and to outcomes. Agreement reported (Cohen's kappa on the four classes and on recoverable = I+R vs not).

## Measures
Per task: share recoverable (I+R), share A. Task class: **recoverable** if ≥ 50% I+R by the mean of the two raters, else **open**.

## Predictions (written before rating)
- Overall: 35-55% of requirements I+R, 25-40% C, 15-30% A.
- The D3 statement gain (N32 and H36, per pass, within task) is present in both task classes; larger on open tasks.

## Reading rules
1. **Agent-side reading supported** (thesis as written): ≥ 50% of requirements I+R overall, and the issue-only statement arms (D4/D5) gain ≤ +1 on recoverable tasks: the information was derivable but neither writer nor agent derived it.
2. **Benchmark-side reading** (thesis reworded to test arbitrariness): ≥ 50% of requirements C+A and the D3 gain concentrated on open tasks (gain on recoverable tasks ≤ 1/3 of the gain on open tasks).
3. **Mixed:** report both shares and the split; the paper states both mechanisms with their sizes.
4. Rater agreement κ < 0.4 on recoverable vs not: the classification is unreliable; report it as such and make no claim from it.
