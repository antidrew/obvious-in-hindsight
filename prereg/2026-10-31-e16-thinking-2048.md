# E16: thinking budget 4096 → 2048 (speed for Kaggle's 12 h, without a wall-clock cap)

Written 2026-10-31, before any run. One change vs agent_e10: `thinking_budget: 2048` (was 4096). Thinking stays on.

Observations: Kaggle time is the binding constraint (e2b over 12 h in 2 of 3 draws; the 5-min wall cap scored 0.06).
Task time is mostly decoding: e10 rb ~10,200 output tokens/task over 26.9 model calls (≈ 200 of 255 s at ~50 tok/s).
3.0% of calls exceed 2048 completion tokens; capping every call at 2048 removes ≤ 11.0% of output tokens (upper bound:
completion tokens include tool-call arguments, which the thinking budget does not limit). Idris v24 is ~15% faster at
equal quality with ~13% fewer output tokens (E13).
Hypothesis: the longest thoughts are mostly churn on hard steps; halving the ceiling shortens tasks without losing solves.
Assumptions: vLLM enforces thinking_token_budget (reasoning_config is set); a capped thought still ends in a valid call.

Predictions (OOD-45 × 2 + rb64 × 2, interleaved with e10):
1. Mechanism: output tokens per task −5% to −11% on rb; mean task time −4% to −10%.
2. Outcome: solved within −1.0/pass pooled over both sets (no loss).
Decision: adopt as the Kaggle base if (1) time ≤ −4% on rb and (2) pooled Δsolved ≥ −1.0 per pass with OOD ≥ −1.5.
Reject if solved drops more (thinking ceiling matters on hard steps), and note it for the paper (C: thinking budget).

## Outcome (2026-11-01): rejected
OOD-45: e10 39.5, e16 37.0 per pass (−2.5); rb64: e10 24.0, e16 21.5 (−2.5). Both beyond the −1.0 rule.
Mechanism: rb output tokens/task 10,012 → 8,979 (−10%), time 243 → 226 s (−7%), as predicted; OOD tokens +5%, time +5%.
The thinking ceiling matters: the 3% longest thoughts carry solves on both sets. Thinking budget stays 4096.
