# Spec LoRA: can an adapter teach Gemma the tests a maintainer writes? (prereg 2026-11-04, before any generation or training)

**Instrument first. It adopts nothing.** A pass licenses one further prereg, not a submission. Design note: `reports/rethink-2026-11-02/spec-lora.md`. Job: `queue/held/80-spec-lora.sh`, not released.

## Observation
- O3 (gold tests in the prompt): N32 1.0 → 12.5, M19 12.5 → 15.5 (`kb/analysis-2026-11-02-competition.md` section 6). Of 14 flips, 5 are arbitrary (`o3-contract.md`, 4-7 by labels), so issue-derived tests reach at most 6-7 of 32.
- Same kill score on the 14 flips, non-D: Claude 5 of 9, 2-7 by sensitivity (`o3b-gate.md`); Gemma with delivery fixed 1.0 of 9 (`results/score-o3g2a20261103.txt`). The decisive case was never named in 11 of 18 attempts (`o3g-traces.md`, O3g, one rater).
- v19 and E7 imitated whole trajectories: loss fell equally on successes and failures (`kb/lora-and-teachers.md`). Forum SFT is null too (`kb/discussions.md`). No sub-skill target has been tried.
- **O3b's tests have never run through the solver.** Their value is a kill count, not a solve count.

## Hypothesis
Which case to test is partly a learnable general skill. Maintainer tests from public PRs teach it across repos, so tests written by an adapter catch more of the solver's wrong patches than the base model's do.

## Assumptions
1. **Learnable, not repo memory.** Upstream names and values cannot be learned. Dropping tests that use fix-only names (17% of extracted rows, 1,122 → 930; see Data) removes only the name part: 4 of 5 D flips pass that filter, so it does not remove arbitrary content. Most likely point of failure.
2. **Selection.** Standalone extraction keeps 34% of rows and favours simple tests: no conftest fixtures, no test-package imports. 47 of 109 bench tasks have such tests.
3. **Location oracle.** Prompts show base code of the files gold touches, in training and on the bench. O2 says location is not the wall, but a pass is conditional on it.
4. **Thinking off.** Targets carry no thought, avoiding `build_rft.py`'s template trap. Deployment thinks. `base_on` guards the comparison; adapter with thinking on is unmeasured here.
5. **Kill is not solve.** Scoring keeps only tests that pass on gold, a filter that cannot ship; the gold-pass guard partly covers this.
6. **Training works as before.** `train/train_lora.py` trained on these exact 4-bit QAT weights twice (v19, E7); longest example here 8,215 tokens, under the 10k measured at 26 GB.

## Bench
`scripts/spec_kill_bench.py`, frozen in `results/spec-lora/bench/FROZEN.sha256` (`1f07a16f…`, flips `0e0fa02a…`). From the 242 measured tasks minus the 14 flips: rate under 0.5, at least one stored unresolved patch editing non-test Python (e18 r1-3 for rb64, e2b p1-4 for the pool), up to 2 distinct. **N = 109: 88 N32-like (rate under 0.1), 88 with two patches.** Their repos are outside training by construction. The 14 flips are read separately, O3g's controls, same score; they do not enter the rule.

Arms, tool-less single shot, e18's sampling: `base_off` ×2, `adapter_off` ×2, `base_on` ×1. Score: `score_o3g.score_task` (O3b's strict rule, rig hash-pinned): a task kills if every stored patch fails a test that passes on gold and fails on base. Paired per task on the mean of draws; one-sided sign-flip, 20,000.

**Power** (simulated, `results/spec-lora/power.txt`, base 15%, N 109, 2 draws): SE 0.025. P(rule passes) at true delta 0: under 0.1%; +5: 2%; +10: 30%; +15: 76%; +20: 96%. It separates 0 from +15, not from +10.

## Predictions
Stage 0: base kill 5-15%, valid sets 20-40%, flips at most 2 of 9, base_on above base_off. Mechanism: fails, gap ratio about 1.0-1.2. Bench: delta 0 to +5. **P(mechanism passes) about 30%; P(bench passes) about 10%; P(live arm then gains ≥ 2 of 64 inside the time limit) about 2% overall.** Judgement, not measurement.

## Decision rules
**Go conditions before release** (any failing means do not release):
1. E23 finished (the job also refuses otherwise).
2. O3b's tests through the solver: on the 14 flips × 2, non-D solves at least 3 of 9 strictly above control. Flips are upper-biased, so a low reading is strong. F51 × 2 as `o3-contract.md` registered is better but needs Claude tests for 37 more tasks. If a strong model's tests do not move the solver, teaching Gemma has no payoff.
3. Candidate-013's draw does not leave the flat time model standing with no slack.

**Stage 0 (no training):** best of base arms kills ≥ 30%: stop, a prompt suffices, own prereg. `base_off` valid sets under 15%: stop, the format fails and says nothing about skill.

**Mechanism, before any bench** (`mech`, 65 held-out-repo examples, same targets with another row's issue): pass if matched NLL falls and gap(mismatched − matched) under the adapter ≥ 1.25 × base's, base gap above 0. A style-only adapter lowers both equally. Fail: LoRA closes for this target.

**Kill rule, all required:** delta ≥ +10 points, p ≤ 0.05, N32-like delta above 0, gold-pass share no more than 10 points below base, adapter kill at or above `base_on`. Anything else: **spec LoRA closes**, no second adapter, no prompt or rank retry without a new observation. Flaky-free delta is read with the verdict.

## What a pass does and does not license
**Does:** the adapter in the O3g2 flow on the flips and F51, thinking on, then at most one live arm under its own prereg, with a Kaggle runtime prediction. **Does not:** any claim about solves, Kaggle, or the base rate on hidden tasks. Time: 15-20% per call is forum 751596, unmeasured by us; slack is about 20% (`inputs.md`), 33-43 s per task on F51 (`2026-11-03-o3g-pilot.md`). A root adapter spends it all. A spec subagent pays on few calls but needs an AgentTool handoff, which cost E19 restarts, plus transcribing code; O3g2's flow alone takes 174-195 s. No LoRA has been drawn on Kaggle. A zero-init draw comes first (`judge-final.md`).

## Do not do this at all if
O3b does not move the solver; or the time model leaves no slack; or the GPU is needed for a lever already passing its rule before the Nov 9 paper freeze.
